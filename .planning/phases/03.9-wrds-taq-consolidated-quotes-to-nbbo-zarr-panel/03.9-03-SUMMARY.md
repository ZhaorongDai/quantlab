# Phase 03.9 Plan 03: SQL-shaped volume guard Summary

SqlVolumeGuard prices a WRDS pull from per-day `count(*)` row counts. It refuses a pull that goes over the 20 GiB raw-byte ceiling (the same value as `UniverseCatalog.MAX_RAW_BYTES`) or the 700M raw-row ceiling. A refusal names the longest date prefix that fits. `--force-volume` skips the refusal but the numbers are still computed. `print_sql_volume_estimate` in `quantlab/utils/cli.py` renders the result.

## What was built

- `quantlab/acquisition/sql_volume.py`: `SqlVolumeGuard` with `MAX_RAW_BYTES = 20 GiB`, `DEFAULT_BYTES_PER_ROW = 30` (an assumption until the plan-08 live smoke measures it), `MAX_RAW_ROWS = 700_000_000` and `CEILINGS`, plus `estimate()` and `assert_acquisition_volume_fits()`.
  - Knobs `max_raw_bytes`, `max_raw_rows` and `bytes_per_row` are read from a kwargs mapping, and each must be a positive number.
  - Day keys are sorted before the prefix search.
  - A refusal lists every crossed ceiling with its constant and its kwargs key. It then gives either `--end-date <fitting>` or "no single day fits — narrow --symbols/--universe", and mentions `--force-volume`.
  - The module imports only `collections.abc`.
- `quantlab/utils/cli.py`: `print_sql_volume_estimate(estimate, *, forced=False, print_fn=print) -> dict`. It follows the same shape as `print_volume_estimate`. It prints no credential and needs no connection.
