**1. In/out-of-sample delta column in the metric table**

> Add a column showing `out_of_sample - in_sample` to the report's metric table.
>
> **The design point that must be settled first:** the table is *generic* — rows are derived by walking whatever keys the metrics mapping carries at render time (`quantlab/utils/backtest_report.py`, `_BLOCKS = ("whole", "in_sample", "out_of_sample")`). A delta column therefore cannot assume numbers:
>
> - duration metrics (`Avg Winning Trade Duration`, `Max Drawdown Duration`) are rendered as **strings** by `to_jsonable` (`pd.Timedelta` → str, `NaT` → null);
> - ratio metrics (Sharpe, Calmar, Profit Factor) are not meaningfully subtractable;
> - a key present in one block and absent from the other must not produce a bogus 0.
>
> Skip by type rather than trying to subtract everything. Do not hardcode a metric list — that genericity is load-bearing and is locked behind behavioural tests (`test_a_mapping_with_every_shipped_key_deleted_still_renders`).

> **RESEARCH CORRECTION (see Pitfall 1).** The *intent* — skip by type, never hardcode a metric list — is correct and is honoured throughout this document. One factual premise is not: the report is handed the **raw** in-memory `metrics` dict, **not** the `to_jsonable`-converted one, so at render time durations are live `pd.Timedelta` objects and missing durations are `pd.NaT`, never strings and never `None`. A predicate written against the string/`None` shapes would skip nothing it was meant to skip and would mis-handle `NaT`. Measured below.

**2. Monthly-return heatmap**

> A year × month matrix, sourced from the same `returns` series the existing `monthly_return` bars use. That series is grouped with `index.to_period("M")` — **not** a resample alias, because the `M`/`ME` alias was renamed across pandas versions while `to_period` is stable. Keep that.
>
> Nothing named `heatmap` exists in the module today (verified: grep count 0).

**3. Positions-only trade metrics + `order_count` in `whole`**

> **User decision (2026-09-16):** "如果 positions.Total Trades 更好 就不要用 Total Trades 了".
>
> Today `_engine_stats` emits BOTH: the lot-level (`exittrades`) set at the top level and a `positions` nested block (quick task 260915-udx). This phase switches the top level to the positions view and stops emitting the lot-level set.
>
> **Why it matters, measured on two independent real runs:**
>
> | run | lot-level Win Rate | position-level Win Rate |
> |---|---|---|
> | 2025 single year | 57.27% | 50.74% |
> | 2020–2026 six years | 57.27% | 50.81% |
>
> A systematic **~6.5 point overstatement**, consistent across both — not sample noise. Cause: equal-weight target-percent rebalancing means only a *winner* ever needs trimming back to its 0.5% target, and vectorbt's exit-trades view records every partial trim as its own closed trade. Confirming detail: the *losing* side is nearly unaffected (−6.28% vs −6.32%) while the winning side is badly distorted — exactly what "only winners get trimmed" predicts.
>
> **The companion change is not optional.** The `whole` block currently has **no execution-activity count at all** — `order_count` / `traded_notional` exist only in the sliced `in_sample` / `out_of_sample` blocks (`_period_record_stats`, around `quantlab/base/backtest.py:1479-1530`). Dropping the lot-level trades would leave the whole-window view with no answer to "how many fills actually happened". `Total Fees Paid` and `turnover` survive and cover cost, but not count.
>
> **API facts, measured:** vectorbt is **1.1.0**. `pf.replace(trades_type="positions")` is the only correct switch and is already in use. Two things that do NOT work and must not be reintroduced: `pf.stats(settings=dict(trades_type=...))` (the string never appears in `Portfolio.stats`) and `pf.get_trades(trades_type=...)` (its signature is `(group_by=None, **kwargs)` — it reads the *instance* attribute). Mutating the global `vbt.settings` is rejected: it leaks across the process.

## Summary

This phase touches three files of an already-working, heavily-locked reporting path. Nothing here needs new libraries, new architecture, or new abstract hooks — it needs precision about types, about where a plotly axis is shared, and about which of ~15 behavioural tests each change turns red.

The single most important finding is a **correction to a premise in CONTEXT.md**: `quantlab/base/backtest.py` writes `to_jsonable(metrics)` to `metrics.json` but hands `write_backtest_report` the *same raw in-memory* `metrics` object. `to_jsonable` returns a new structure and does not mutate its input, so the report's `_cell` sees live `pd.Timestamp`, `pd.Timedelta`, `pd.NaT` and `np.float64('nan')` — not the strings and `null`s that land in `metrics.json`. A delta predicate written against the JSON shapes would be wrong on exactly the metrics CONTEXT.md warns about. The right predicate is `numbers.Real` (stdlib, no numpy import) with an explicit `bool` exclusion and a finiteness check; measured, it admits `int`/`float`/`np.int64`/`np.float64` and rejects `np.bool_`, `str`, `pd.Timedelta`, `pd.Timestamp`, `pd.NaT` and `None`.

The second finding removes an attractive but wrong design for the heatmap. The existing figure is `make_subplots(rows=3, shared_xaxes=True)`. Adding the heatmap as a fourth row was measured to make plotly set `matches='x4'` on `xaxis`, `xaxis2` and `xaxis3` — tying the three datetime rows to the heatmap's **categorical** month axis. The heatmap therefore belongs in its own `go.Figure` rendered as a second div with `include_plotlyjs=False` (~8 KB). Measured bonus: both existing trace parsers (`tests/test_backtest_report.py:298`, `tests/test_backtest_persistence.py:696`) decode only the **first** `Plotly.newPlot(`, so all four exact-trace-set locks and the pixel-budget lock stay green untouched — which is also why the plan must ship a *second-div* parser or the heatmap will be shipped untested.

The third finding is that the positions switch is cleaner than expected in one respect and messier in another. Cleaner: one call to `pf.replace(trades_type="positions").stats(metrics=STATS_METRICS)` is sufficient — all 14 portfolio-level metrics were measured **byte-identical** to the current top level, so no second `stats()` call and no drift risk. Messier: `_period_record_stats`'s `closed_trade_count` / `open_trade_count` are derived from `SimulationResult.trades`, which `_simulate` builds from `pf.trades.records_readable` (exit trades). Switching only `whole` breaks two reconciliation identities that `tests/test_backtest_metrics.py:518-522` asserts today. The fix is to switch `SimulationResult.trades` to `pf.positions.records_readable` in the same change — measured safe: all six fields `_simulate` reads exist in the positions frame with the same `Closed`/`Open` status vocabulary.

**Primary recommendation:** Three independent plans. (1) Delta column — pure leaf edit to `backtest_report.py`, `numbers.Real` + non-`bool` + `math.isfinite` on both operands, dash otherwise; no `pd.Timedelta` arithmetic unless the user asks for it. (2) Heatmap — new `_monthly_heatmap_div()` in the leaf, rendered as a second plotly div below the main figure, month index `period.month - 1`. (3) Positions switch — one `replace()` call covering all of `STATS_METRICS`, `SimulationResult.trades` switched to the positions records in the same commit, `whole["order_count"] = int(simulation.orders.sizes.get("order", 0))` added in `_compute_metrics`, nested `positions` block deleted, notes and `example/backtest.md` rewritten.

### Pattern 1: the delta column — dispatch on the RAW render-time type

**What:** For each flattened row key, compute `out_of_sample - in_sample` only when both operands are *finite real numbers that are not booleans*; render a dash otherwise.

**Why this exact predicate.** Measured type census of what `_metrics_section` actually receives (raw, pre-`to_jsonable`):

| Render-time Python type | Example key | `isinstance(v, numbers.Real)` | `isinstance(v, bool)` | `_cell(v)` renders |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `int` | `Total Trades`, `order_count` | True | False | `'31'` |
| `float` / `np.float64` | `Win Rate [%]`, `Sharpe Ratio` | True | False | `'44.8'` |
| `np.float64('nan')` | `Annualized Volatility [%]` on a 1-bar slice | True | False | `'—'` |
| `float('inf')` | possible on degenerate ratios | True | False | `'—'` |
| `bool` | none today; possible in a future sub-dict | **True** | **True** | `'true'` |
| `np.bool_` | none today | **False** | False | `'True'` |
| `pd.Timedelta` | `Period`, `Max Drawdown Duration`, `Avg Winning Trade Duration` | False | False | `'117 days 00:00:00'` |
| `pd.Timestamp` | `Start`, `End` | False | False | `'2024-01-02 00:00:00'` |
| `pd.NaT` | `Max Drawdown Duration` on a 1-bar slice | False | False | `'NaT'` |
| `str` | `Status`-like values | False | False | `'Closed'` |
| `None` | absent key (via `.get`) | False | False | `'—'` |

`[VERIFIED: measured this session by importing `_cell` from `quantlab.utils.backtest_report` and evaluating each case]`

Measured naive-subtraction behaviour, which is why each exclusion is needed:

| Operand pair | `b - a` | Why it must be excluded |
|---|---|---|
| `float - float` | `np.float64(2.0)` | ✅ keep |
| `int - int` | `3` | ✅ keep |
| `nan - float` | `nan` → `_cell` → `'—'` | Harmless but must be *deliberate*: the finiteness check makes it a dash for a stated reason rather than by luck |
| `bool - bool` | `1` | ❌ `True - False == 1` renders as a number; `bool` is an `int` subclass so **it must be excluded before the numeric check** |
| `str - str` | **raises `TypeError`** | ❌ an exception here deletes the entire run directory |
| `None - float` | **raises `TypeError`** | ❌ same |
| `Timedelta - Timedelta` | `Timedelta('3 days')` → `'3 days 00:00:00'` | Mechanically fine; a *semantic* choice — see Open Question 1 |
| `Timestamp - Timestamp` | `Timedelta('60 days')` | ❌ a "delta" on `Start` is meaningless; excluded by the same predicate that admits neither |

**The recommended predicate (leaf-safe, no numpy):**

```python
import numbers   # stdlib; math is already imported at quantlab/utils/backtest_report.py:67

def _delta(later: object, earlier: object) -> object:
    """`later - earlier` when both are finite real numbers, else None.

    Dispatches on TYPE, never on metric name: the table's row set is derived
    from the mapping at render time, so a rule keyed to `Sharpe Ratio` would
    be dead the day the metric set is replaced -- and this runs inside the
    run's staging directory, where an exception costs the whole run.

    `bool` is tested FIRST because it is an `int` subclass: without it,
    `True - False` would render as the number 1. `numbers.Real` is used rather
    than `(int, float)` so a numpy scalar is admitted (`isinstance(np.int64(5),
    int)` is False); `np.bool_` is not a `numbers.Real`, so it needs no case.
    `pd.Timedelta`, `pd.Timestamp`, `pd.NaT`, `str` and `None` are all outside
    `numbers.Real` and fall through to None, which the caller renders as a dash.
    """
    for value in (later, earlier):
        if isinstance(value, bool) or not isinstance(value, numbers.Real):
            return None
        if not math.isfinite(value):
            return None
    return later - earlier
```

```python
def _monthly_grid(monthly: pd.Series) -> tuple[list[int], list[list[float | None]]]:
    """`monthly` (a PeriodIndex of month periods) -> (years, z[year][month]).

    `period.month` is 1-BASED, so the column index is `period.month - 1`. An
    off-by-one here is invisible: every cell still renders, the picture still
    looks like a heatmap, and nothing raises -- the numbers are simply in the
    wrong month. That is why the lock below asserts a single known return in a
    single known month rather than a shape.
    """
    years = sorted({period.year for period in monthly.index})
    grid: list[list[float | None]] = [[None] * 12 for _ in years]
    row = {year: i for i, year in enumerate(years)}
    for period, value in monthly.items():

    return years, grid
```

### Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Hardcoding a metric list anywhere in the report leaf.** The genericity is locked by `test_a_mapping_with_every_shipped_key_deleted_still_renders` and by the module docstring's own reasoning (`quantlab/utils/backtest_report.py:26-34`). A ratio-metric exclusion list would be exactly this.
- **Adding a fourth subplot row for the heatmap.** Measured to couple the datetime axes to a categorical one — see Pitfall 6.
- **A `try/except` around the new report code.** The module's established discipline is *explicit* interception (`.get` everything, `if not span: return`, `if stamp not in equity.index: continue`), never a blanket catch. `_drawdown_span`'s docstring states this as D-6: 不包 try/except. Follow it.
- **Reintroducing `pf.stats(settings=dict(trades_type=...))` or `pf.get_trades(trades_type=...)`.** CONTEXT.md measured both as silently ignored. The failure is invisible: you get a byte-identical copy that looks fine.
- **Mutating `vbt.settings` globally.** Leaks across every Portfolio in the process.
- **Adding a run-directory file.** Four exact-set assertions. The heatmap must live inside `report.html`, not in a sibling file.
- **Wrapping a verify command in `timeout`/`gtimeout`.** Neither exists on this machine; the command exits 0 having run nothing — a false green. (CONTEXT.md, measured.)
- **Writing a note containing `<`, `>`, `&`, `"` or `'`.** `tests/test_backtest_persistence.py:960` asserts, verbatim: `assert not set(note) & set("<>&\"'"), note` `[VERIFIED: tests/test_backtest_persistence.py:958-960]`.

## Don't Hand-Roll

| Problem | Don't Build | Use Instead | Why |
|---------|-------------|-------------|-----|
| Position-level trade statistics | A manual round-trip aggregator over `simulation.trades` (group by symbol, stitch entries to flats) | `pf.replace(trades_type="positions")` / `pf.positions` | vectorbt already reconciles partial fills, reversals and direction; the measured lot-vs-position divergence (31 → 10 records) is exactly the accounting this would have to reproduce |
| Monthly compounding | A resample alias (`"M"` / `"ME"`) | `index.to_period("M")` + `groupby().prod()` | The alias was renamed across pandas versions; `to_period` reads the same in both. Already the module's rule (`quantlab/utils/backtest_report.py:353-357`) |
| A numeric type test | `str(v).replace('.','').isdigit()` or a `try: float(v)` | `numbers.Real` + explicit `bool` exclusion + `math.isfinite` | `float("nan")` and `float("inf")` both pass a `try: float(v)`; `bool` passes everything; a string `'31'` would be *coerced* rather than skipped, silently differencing text |
| JSON-safe serialization | Per-call conversion | `quantlab/utils/jsonable.py:to_jsonable` | Already the single gate for every persisted artifact; it raises `TypeError` on an unknown type rather than stringifying (`quantlab/utils/jsonable.py:121-125`) |
| HTML escaping | Manual `.replace("<", "&lt;")` | the module's `_escape` (`html.escape`) | Already applied to every interpolated value; the div is plotly's and is inserted verbatim by design |
| Heatmap rendering | A hand-built HTML `<table>` with background colours | `go.Heatmap` | Gets hover, colour scale, `zmid=0` diverging centring and null-cell gaps for free — and plotly is already the page's renderer |

**Key insight:** every "hand-roll" temptation in this phase is a *reimplementation of an accounting or formatting rule that already exists one layer down*, and each one fails silently rather than loudly — a wrong win rate, a month shifted by one, a string differenced as a number. None of them raises.

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: the report receives RAW metrics, not the `to_jsonable` output

**What goes wrong:** A delta predicate is written against `str` durations and `None` for missing values (as CONTEXT.md describes `metrics.json`), and then skips nothing it was supposed to skip — or worse, a `pd.NaT` slips through a `value is None` check.

**Why it happens:** The two artifacts are written five lines apart and look symmetric. `quantlab/base/backtest.py:1799-1816`, verbatim:

```python

                run_dir / "metrics.json", to_jsonable(metrics), indent=2
            )
            drawdown_span = self._drawdown_span(simulation)
            write_backtest_report(
                simulation.value,
                run_dir / "report.html",
                in_sample_range=metrics.get("in_sample_range"),
                notes=self._report_notes(),
                title=name,
                summary=self._report_summary(
                    simulation, metrics, drawdown_span=drawdown_span
                ),
                metrics=metrics,
```

`[VERIFIED: quantlab/base/backtest.py:1799-1812]` — `to_jsonable(metrics)` for the JSON, bare `metrics` for the report. `to_jsonable` builds and returns a new structure (`quantlab/utils/jsonable.py:93-104`), so the original is untouched. The same asymmetry exists on the CV path: `quantlab/base/backtest.py:1957-1970` writes `to_jsonable(metrics)` and passes `metrics=metrics["stitched"]`.

**How to avoid:** Write the predicate against the raw types (table in Pattern 1) and *prove it* with a leaf test that feeds `pd.Timedelta`, `pd.NaT`, `pd.Timestamp` and `np.float64('nan')` directly to `write_backtest_report(metrics=...)` — not through a run.

**Warning signs:** a delta test that passes only when the fixture uses plain Python floats; a `'NaT'` string appearing in a page cell.

**Pre-existing blemish surfaced by this work (not necessarily in scope):** `_cell(pd.NaT)` renders `'NaT'` `[VERIFIED: measured]`, while `metrics.json` carries `null` for the same metric. So on a one-bar in-sample slice the page today says `NaT` where the JSON says `null`. Raise with the user rather than silently fixing it inside another change (Open Question 3).

### Pitfall 2: `bool` is an `int` subclass

**What goes wrong:** `isinstance(True, (int, float))` is `True`, so a boolean metric is differenced and `True - False` renders as `1`.

**Why it happens:** Python's numeric tower. `numbers.Real` does not save you — measured, `isinstance(True, numbers.Real)` is also `True`.

**How to avoid:** test `isinstance(value, bool)` **before** the numeric check and return early. `np.bool_` needs no case: measured `isinstance(np.bool_(True), numbers.Real)` is `False`.

**Warning signs:** none at runtime — the page just shows a plausible `1`. The existing `_cell` gets this right (`quantlab/utils/backtest_report.py:440-441`, `bool` branch before `int`); mirror that ordering.

### Pitfall 3: a key present in one block and absent from the other

**What goes wrong:** `columns[name].get(key)` returns `None` for the missing side, and `None - 3.0` raises `TypeError` **inside the staging directory** — deleting the entire run.

**Why it happens:** `_metrics_section` builds the row set as the union across blocks (`quantlab/utils/backtest_report.py:468-478`), so `whole`-only rows (all 27 `STATS_METRICS` keys) are present with two `None` slice operands. And a `None` block becomes `{}` by design (line 465), producing an all-`None` column.

**How to avoid:** the predicate returns `None` on any non-`Real` operand *before* attempting arithmetic — `None` is not `numbers.Real`, so this is already covered. Add an explicit test for the `whole`-only row case and for `in_sample=None`.

**Warning signs:** the only signal is a lost run directory. There is no partial failure mode.

### Pitfall 4: `simulation.orders` can have no `order` dimension

**What goes wrong:** `simulation.orders.sizes["order"]` raises `KeyError` on an order-less simulation.

**Why it happens:** `tests/test_backtest_metrics.py:327` passes `orders=xr.Dataset()`; a real window with no rebalance fill would do the same.

**How to avoid:** `.sizes.get("order", 0)`, matching the two existing call sites (`quantlab/base/backtest.py:1425`, `:1497`).

**Warning signs:** a unit test that constructs `SimulationResult` directly goes red while every end-to-end test stays green.

### The heatmap figure

```python
def _monthly_heatmap_div(returns: xr.DataArray | None) -> str:
    """A year x month grid of compounded monthly returns, as its own div.

    Its own figure rather than a fourth row of the main one: the main figure is
    `shared_xaxes=True`, and a fourth row makes plotly set `matches='x4'` on the
    three datetime axes -- tying the equity, drawdown and monthly-bar axes to
    this chart's CATEGORICAL month axis. Measured on plotly 5.24.1.

    `include_plotlyjs=False`: the main div already loaded plotly.js from the
    CDN, so this adds about 8 KB rather than a second library copy.

    Same series and same compounding as the bar panel, via `_monthly_series`:
    two panels of the same numbers must not be able to drift apart.

    Returns an empty string when there is nothing to draw, exactly as the bar
    panel returns early -- this runs inside the run's staging directory, where
    an exception costs the whole run.
    """
    monthly = _monthly_series(returns)
    if monthly is None or monthly.empty:
        return ""
    years, z = _monthly_grid(monthly)
    fig = go.Figure(
        go.Heatmap(
            z=z,
            x=[f"{month:02d}" for month in range(1, 13)],
            y=[str(year) for year in years],
            name="monthly_return_heatmap",
            colorscale="RdBu",
            zmid=0.0,
            showscale=True,
            hovertemplate="%{y}-%{x}<br>%{z:.2%}<extra></extra>",
        )
    )
    # Earliest year on top: the conventional reading order for this chart.
    fig.update_yaxes(autorange="reversed")
    fig.update_layout(height=max(180, 40 * len(years) + 120), margin={"t": 30})
    return fig.to_html(full_html=False, include_plotlyjs=False)
```

### The two failure modes that would otherwise pass silently

These are the reason this section exists, and each needs a *specific* test shape, not coverage:

1. **A delta column that silently drops a metric it should have differenced.** A test asserting "every delta cell is either a number or a dash" passes when the predicate returns `None` for everything. The lock must be a **census**: on a real run, assert the *exact set* of row keys that carry a delta, and assert its size (expected: the 13 returns-accessor floats + `order_count`, `fees_paid`, `traded_notional`, `closed_trade_count`, `open_trade_count`, and the three `turnover.*` — 21 rows), **and** assert that `Start`, `End`, `Period` and `Max Drawdown Duration` are dashed. A pure-count assertion is not enough either: it survives a predicate that differences the wrong four. Assert the set, both halves.

   *Mutation to run against it:* change `_delta` to `return None` unconditionally, and separately to drop the `math.isfinite` check. Both must turn a test red. If the first passes, the test is vacuous.

2. **A heatmap whose month alignment is off by one.** Shape assertions (12 columns, N rows, right number of non-null cells) all pass under the off-by-one. The lock must feed **one** return in **one** month and assert the x label of the non-null cell — measured: March 2024 → index `2` → label `'03'`; the off-by-one variant yields `'04'` with nothing else differing.

   *Mutation to run against it:* `period.month - 1` → `period.month`. Must turn that test red and ideally nothing else.

   A second, subtler alignment bug: the **year** row index. Feed two returns in the *same month of different years* and assert both rows.

3. **(Bonus, item 3) A `positions` switch that did not actually switch.** CONTEXT.md and the existing test file both record that per-call `trades_type` is silently ignored, producing a byte-identical copy. The existing defence is the `RotateOneOutEqualWeight` fixture (`tests/test_backtest_engine.py:676-708`), whose rebalances trim without closing so the two views genuinely diverge. **Keep that fixture.** Its docstring states the reason: on a fixture where every holding opens and closes once, the two views coincide and the lock is vacuous. **The security control that matters here is availability, not confidentiality:** a drawing bug costs a trained model and a completed backtest. Treat "the new code cannot raise on any input the mapping can carry" as a security requirement, not a robustness nicety.

## Open Questions

1. **Should ratio metrics be excluded from the delta column?**
   - What we know: CONTEXT.md says Sharpe/Calmar/Profit Factor "are not meaningfully subtractable". Measured: they are plain `float`s, type-indistinguishable from `Total Return [%]` and `Win Rate [%]`, which *are* sensibly differenced.
   - What's unclear: excluding them requires naming them — i.e. a hardcoded metric list, which CONTEXT.md forbids in the same breath and which a behavioural test locks.
   - Recommendation: **difference everything finite and non-boolean**, and state the operation in the column header (`out_of_sample - in_sample`) so it reads as arithmetic rather than as a verdict. Escalate to the user before implementing; if they insist on skipping ratios, the genericity constraint must be explicitly relaxed and the lock's rationale rewritten — that is a phase-scope change, not a task detail.

2. **Does the positions switch include deleting the nested `positions` block and switching `SimulationResult.trades`?**
   - What we know: CONTEXT.md says "switches the top level to the positions view and stops emitting the lot-level set". Measured: leaving `SimulationResult.trades` as exit trades breaks two reconciliation identities at `tests/test_backtest_metrics.py:518-522`; the positions frame is schema-compatible with what `_simulate` reads.
   - What's unclear: whether the user wants the *slice* counts (`closed_trade_count`, `open_trade_count`) to change meaning too.
   - Recommendation: yes to both — one coherent view. Confirm with the user, because it silently changes two numbers they may have been tracking.

3. **`_cell(pd.NaT)` renders the literal `'NaT'` on the page while `metrics.json` carries `null`.**
   - What we know: measured, and reachable — a one-bar in-sample slice yields `Max Drawdown Duration = NaT`.
   - What's unclear: whether it is in scope. It is pre-existing, and the delta work puts eyes on exactly this code.
   - Recommendation: a two-line fix (`if value is pd.NaT` → dash, or `pd.isna(value)` guarded to non-numerics) filed as a separate quick task, not folded into item 1 — folding it would make item 1's test story ambiguous about what it proved.

4. **The rendered consequence of `matches='x4'` was inferred from layout JSON, not from a rendered figure.**
   - What we know: the linkage is measured verbatim in the emitted layout. `kaleido` is not installed, so `full_figure_for_development()` cannot resolve the axis types in-process.
   - Recommendation: do not install kaleido for this. The second-div design sidesteps the question entirely, and the standing "a human must open a report.html" item covers visual confirmation.

5. **Keep the monthly bar row as well as the heatmap?**
   - Recommendation: keep both (they answer different questions, ~8 KB cost). Ask the user in one line during discuss/plan; if they want one panel, dropping the bars is the cheaper change and would free the 3rd row for the heatmap — but would then re-open Pitfall 6.

6. **Does the delta column belong in `metrics.json` too?**
   - What we know: CONTEXT.md scopes it to "the report's metric table". Adding it to `metrics.json` would be a derived value with no consumer and would widen the wandb summary.
   - Recommendation: report only. Do not persist it.
